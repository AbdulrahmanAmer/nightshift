The last ferry left the harbour at nine, and Mara was the only passenger. The deck lights flickered as the engine coughed into life.

Halfway across, the captain stopped the boat. He said nothing, only pointed at the water, where a second set of lights moved under the surface, keeping pace with them.

Mara asked what it was. The captain said the lights had followed the ferry every night for thirty years, and that they belonged to the ferry that sank before this one was built.

When they reached the far shore, the lights turned back toward the open sea. The captain tied the ropes and switched off the engine.

On the dock, Mara found a ticket stub, dry and folded, dated the night the old ferry went down. It had her name on it.
